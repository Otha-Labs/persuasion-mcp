# That starter you threw out probably wasn't dead.

**Sourdough Without Fear** is a $49 guide for total beginners. It takes you from an empty jar to your first good loaf in about two weeks, in plain English.

Most sourdough guides are written by people who've forgotten what it's like not to know what "autolyse" means. So they skip the parts that trip beginners up.

Maybe you've already tried. You fed a starter for six days until it smelled like nail polish remover, so you poured it down the sink. Or you made it all the way to baking day and pulled a dense, pale brick out of the oven. Or maybe you just watched a 40-minute video, heard "bulk ferment" eleven times, and quietly decided bread wasn't for you.

Here's the good news: none of that means you're bad at this.

That nail-polish smell is a hungry starter asking to be fed. And the brick? Usually that's dough that needed more time to rise. Most first-loaf failures come down to timing, and you can learn to judge timing by looking at your dough.

That's what this book teaches. You'll learn what "ready" looks like at every stage, so you can stop watching the clock and trust your eyes.

## What's inside

- **Week 1: your starter.** What to feed it each day, and how it should look and smell when it's ready to bake with.
- **Your first loaf.** One simple recipe, with photos of the dough at each stage so you're never guessing.
- **Troubleshooting.** Flat loaf, gummy middle, pale crust, a starter that won't bubble. Find your problem, then read the fix.
- **A plain-English glossary.** *Levain* is a scoop of starter. *Hydration* is how wet your dough is. Every explanation in the book is that simple.

You won't need a stand mixer or a proofing box. A jar and a mixing bowl get you started, and your regular oven does the rest.

## Why I wrote it

My first few loaves went in the bin. I didn't need more information. I needed someone to tell me which parts mattered and which I could ignore. That's what this book does.

I can't promise your first loaf will look like the ones on Instagram. I can promise it'll be bread, and you'll know exactly why it turned out the way it did.

**Persuasion techniques used** (from the [Persuasion Taxonomy](https://taxonomy.coppica.com), CC BY 4.0):
- The page opens with [Enumerated Failed Alternatives](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/agitate/enumerated-failed-alternatives): the dead starter, the brick and the video. Each one gets a calm explanation instead of making the reader feel worse.
- The glossary lines use [Jargon Calibration](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/identify/jargon-calibration). They show the book's plain-English tone instead of just claiming it.
- "I can't promise your first loaf will look like the ones on Instagram" is a [Hedged Transformation Promise](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/bond/hedged-transformation-promise). Making a smaller promise makes it easier to believe.
- The free Day 1 recipe uses the [Endowment Effect](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/compel/endowment-effect). It gives the reader a reason to buy now without a fake deadline: once they've started a starter, they'll want to know what to do on Day 2.
