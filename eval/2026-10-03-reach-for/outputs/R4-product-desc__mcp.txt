**What changed**

- **The opening says something unexpected.** "Stop buying T-shirts" is an odd thing for a T-shirt brand to say, so people stop scrolling. It uses [The Enemy of Conventional Wisdom](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/disrupt/enemy-of-conventional-wisdom).
- **The $34 price gets an argument.** Your shoppers are comparing it to $10 tees. The "only cheap until you replace it" line makes the price about how long the shirt lasts instead of what it costs today ([The Hidden Cost Reveal](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/agitate/hidden-cost-reveal)).
- **"Sustainable" now means something.** "One shirt you keep than five you throw out" connects sustainability to durability, so the two claims back each other up instead of sitting in a list ([The Values Declaration](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/bond/values-declaration)).
- **The practical details answer doubts.** "Order your usual" deals with worry about fit, and "No special care" deals with worry about washing.

I checked the draft with the persuasion-taxonomy diagnostic. It covers 8 of the 9 questions shoppers ask before buying. The one it only partly covers is "why buy now?" I didn't make up a deadline or deal, and I kept to what you told me: no fabric weights, reviews or test results. The last three lines are short on purpose so they're easy to scan. The diagnostic marks that list style down, but on a product page it's the right call.
