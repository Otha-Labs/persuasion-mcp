The plan is built around one idea, and the email and ad both serve it.

## Why sales are probably flat

When Instagram sales stay flat for months, the usual cause is that the same followers keep seeing the same product shots and nothing gives them a reason to buy today. You have a reason most brands have to fake: each piece is one of a kind and comes from one kiln load. Next week turns that into a dated event. Your email list gets the first 24 hours, and an ad brings in people who don't follow you yet.

I don't have your numbers, so check two things on Monday. They tell us which problem we're actually solving:
- **Instagram reach and profile visits over the last 3 months.** If they're flat or dropping, the problem is getting seen, and the ad matters most.
- **Site visits from Instagram compared with orders.** If visits held steady but orders didn't, look at checkout first, especially shipping cost. Ceramics are heavy, and a surprise shipping charge at the last step is a common reason people abandon a cart.

## The ad

**Format:** a 15-second vertical video for Reels, Stories and Feed.
- **0–2s:** A plant in a scuffed plastic nursery pot on a shelf. Text on screen: *Still in the plastic pot it came home in?*
- **2–6s:** Hands lift it out and set it into one of your planters.
- **6–11s:** Shots of the wheel, glazing and the kiln. Text: *[24] pieces from one kiln load*
- **11–15s:** The plant back in place in the room. Text: *$38 to $120*

**Setup:**
- **Budget:** about $20–25 a day for 7 days.
- **Audience:** people aged 25–45 interested in houseplants and home decor, plus a lookalike of your past buyers if your ad account has enough of them.

## How we'll judge it

- **Email:** open rate, click rate, and orders in the first 24 hours compared with your last few sends.
- **Ad:** cost per landing-page view and add-to-carts. Seven days and about $150 won't produce enough purchases to judge the ad on sales, so treat it as a test of which hook works, not the whole fix.
- **Overall:** if the email sells well but the ad doesn't, your existing audience is fine and the problem is reach. If both stall, look at the shop and checkout.

## Why the copy is built this way

These are the persuasion techniques the copy uses, from the [Persuasion Taxonomy](https://taxonomy.coppica.com) (CC BY 4.0):
- **[Strange Fact](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/disrupt/strange-fact):** the shrinkage opener.
- **[Small Batch Signal](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/prove/small-batch-signal):** one kiln load as proof of the handwork.
- **[Exclusive Access](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/elevate/exclusive-access):** the list gets a day's head start.
- **[Preemptive Objection](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/resolve/preemptive-objection):** answering fit, drainage, breakage and price before they stop the sale.
- **[Behavioral Mirror Hook](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/identify/behavioral-mirror-hook):** the ad's plastic-pot opener.

I checked both drafts against that framework's copy checklist. Each covers what it needs for its goal, and I revised them so they read less like a list.
