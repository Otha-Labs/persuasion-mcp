## The roast

**Headline: "Empowering teams to do their best work"**

Try swapping the logo. This headline works just as well for Slack, Asana, Notion, a standing desk, an HR consultancy, or a nice office coffee machine. When a line fits every company, it says nothing about yours. "Empowering" is the beige paint of B2B verbs, and "their best work" is what every tool since the stapler has promised. A reader finishes the headline and still doesn't know what Flowly is.

**Subheadline: "the all-in-one platform that streamlines collaboration, boosts productivity, and drives results."**

- **"All-in-one platform"** sounds like you couldn't pick what the product is for.
- **"Streamlines collaboration, boosts productivity, drives results"** is three filled squares on a buzzword bingo card. It also uses the verb-noun, verb-noun, verb-noun pattern that readers now recognize as AI filler and skip.
- **"Drives results."** Which results? Revenue? Fewer meetings? Shipping on time? "Results" is what you write when you don't know.

The subheadline's job is to explain what the headline left out. Here it just adds more fog.

**Button: "Get Started"**

Started with what? Is it a free trial, a sales call, or a credit card form? You're asking for a commitment before saying what it costs, and nothing above the button gives anyone a reason to click.

## The scorecard

I ran it through a copy diagnostic:

- **Required reader questions answered clearly: 0 of 6.** "Is this for me?" and "Why act now?" are only partly answered. "Why should I pay attention?", "How bad is my problem?" and "Can I trust this?" aren't answered at all.
- **Headline: 3.9/10.** It isn't specific, it gives no reason to read on, and it uses "best" without backing it up.
- **Craft: 4/10.** Flagged for "empowering", sentences all the same length, and lines that read like a list instead of building on each other.
- **Biggest problem:** nothing makes the reader stop. They see three nice-sounding lines and keep scrolling.

## How to fix it

1. **Name the problem or the reader.** A specific pain like "status meetings," "lost Slack threads" or "projects that slip" beats any line about empowerment.
2. **Add one piece of proof.** A number, a customer name or a measured result. Right now the hero only makes promises.
3. **Say what the button does and what it costs.** "Start free, no card" or "See a 2-minute demo" tells people what they're agreeing to.
