For your headline I'd go with **"You make the work. Nudge chases the money."**

**Subheadline:** Nudge follows up on late invoices by email and SMS, on a schedule you set, so your creative agency gets paid without you writing a single awkward reminder.

The headline tells agency owners the product is for them, and it splits the work so the money-chasing becomes someone else's job. The subheadline says how it works and takes on the real reason invoices go unchased: asking a client for money feels awkward.

**Alternative to A/B test against it:**

**Your agency did the work. Nudge makes sure the invoice gets paid.**
Polite, persistent reminders go out by email and SMS until the client pays. You keep the relationship, and Nudge does the nagging.

The two pairs make different bets. The first changes how the owner sees the problem: chasing money isn't their job any more. The second leans on the result: the invoice gets paid. Whichever wins tells you which of those matters more to agency owners.

I checked both headlines with the persuasion-taxonomy tools. Both are clearly aimed at agency owners, and a competitor couldn't use either one unchanged because each names Nudge. There's one gap I couldn't fill: the copy promises you'll get paid but gives no proof. If you have a real number, like average days to payment before and after Nudge or the total amount recovered for customers, add it to the subheadline. That will help more than any wording change. I didn't make up a statistic.
