I've planned next week around a small-batch drop: one real reason to buy now, aimed at the people who already like your work, plus one ad to reach people who've never seen it.

## Why sales are probably flat

I don't have your numbers, so this is my best guess. Instagram keeps showing your posts to the same people, and most of them have already decided whether to buy. Your product photos show the work but don't give anyone a reason to buy this week, so "I'll get one eventually" wins.

Three questions also go unanswered for most followers: "Will it fit my plant?", "Will it survive shipping?" and "Why does it cost three times a store pot?"

Before spending anything, check two things words can't fix:
- **Shipping cost:** does it show up before checkout? Ceramics are heavy, and a surprise shipping charge loses sales.
- **Your bio link:** does it go straight to the new pieces?

## Before you send anything

- **Fill in every bracket with a true fact.** I didn't have these details. The breakage promise, the saucers and the next-batch date matter most, because the email asks people to trust exactly those claims. Check the 12% figure against your own clay too; stoneware usually shrinks 10–15%.
- **The checks I ran:** I tested both drafts against a framework of the nine questions readers ask before buying. Two things came back. Neither draft said what the problem costs the reader, so I added the "next time" line to the email and the cropped-out nursery pot to the ad. The ad's sentences were also all about the same length, so I rewrote them to vary.
- **My first subject lines got cut off.** The reason to open was past the ~40 characters a phone shows, so I rewrote the subject to fit.
- **This can't predict sales.** Only the Monday numbers will show whether this works. With ~$100 on the ad, judge it on cost per click rather than purchases; that's too few sales to read.
