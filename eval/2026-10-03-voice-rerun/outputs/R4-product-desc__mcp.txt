**The organic cotton tee that ends up at the top of the drawer.**

Most t-shirts are made to be replaced. A few washes in, the collar ripples, the hem twists, and you're back at the checkout buying another.

Ours is cut from organic cotton, grown without synthetic pesticides or fertilizers. Soft from the first wear. And durable enough to hold up wash after wash, which matters more than it sounds, because the greenest shirt is the one you don't have to buy again.

No special care. Throw it in with the rest of your laundry, order the size you always wear (it fits true to size), and choose from six colors.

We'd rather sell you one good shirt than five forgettable ones.

$34. Start with your favorite color.

---

**What changed**

- **Durability is the main argument now.** Your original listed "durable" as one feature among several. Here it carries the case for "sustainable," because it's the one green claim a shopper can check for themselves. ([Buy It For Life Frame](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/reframe/buy-it-for-life-frame))
- **The $34 gets a comparison.** A shopper who sees $34 compares it to a $12 tee. The opening sets it against a $12 tee you keep replacing instead.
- **Fit and washing now answer doubts.** "Machine washable" and "fits true to size" were spec-sheet items. Now they answer "will it fit?" and "is it a hassle to care for?"

**I didn't invent anything, so three things would make it stronger if you have them:**

1. **A certification, like GOTS.** People who doubt green claims want this more than anything else. One line with a certification would do more than any rewording. The "grown without synthetic pesticides or fertilizers" line is true by definition for certified organic cotton, so please confirm yours qualifies.
2. **A reason to buy today.** Nothing in your facts gives one, and I didn't want to fake urgency. A real multi-pack price, like "3 for $90," would give one honestly.
3. **A returns or fit guarantee, or a review count.** Either would make it less risky to try a $34 tee from a brand they haven't bought before.

Before writing, I planned the copy with the Persuasion Taxonomy tools, then checked the draft with them. The check found no reason-to-buy-now and sentences that all ran the same length. I fixed the sentence lengths. The reason to buy now still depends on the offer details above.
