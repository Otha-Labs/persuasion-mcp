Here's the roast.

**The big problem:** almost any SaaS company could put this hero on its site. Replace "Flowly" with Asana, Notion, Monday, ClickUp or a corporate HR portal and every word still works. After reading it, a visitor still doesn't know what Flowly *is*.

I also ran it through a persuasion diagnostic tool. A signup page needs to answer six questions for the reader, and this hero doesn't clearly answer any of them. It doesn't grab attention, say who it's for, give a reason to believe it, or say what happens when you click.

### Headline: "Empowering teams to do their best work"
- **"Empowering"** is one of the most overused words in corporate copy. Readers skim right past it.
- **"Teams":** which teams? Engineers? Agencies? A 6-person startup or a 6,000-person bank?
- **"Their best work":** what work? It promises a feeling, not a result.
- **It never says what kind of product this is.** Flowly could be a task manager, a chat app, a whiteboard or a meditation program.

### Subheadline: "the all-in-one platform that streamlines collaboration, boosts productivity, and drives results"
- **"All-in-one platform"** for what? All-in-one *what*?
- **"Streamlines collaboration, boosts productivity, and drives results"** is the standard B2B SaaS phrase trio: three verb-noun pairs and no information. "Drives results" could describe a golf cart.
- **No proof:** there's no number, no named customer and no explanation of *how* it makes anyone more productive. It promises a better life and gives no reason to believe it, so readers tune it out.
- **It sounds machine-written.** It reads like a committee or an AI wrote it. A generic word list plus a tidy list of three is exactly what readers have learned to ignore.

### Button: "Get Started"
Started with what? A free trial? A sales call? A form that wants a credit card? When people can't tell what they're signing up for, they hesitate. Say what they get.

### How to fix it
1. **Say who it's for and what's bothering them.** Two ways to open:
   - [The Pointed Question](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/disrupt/question-open): ask something the reader can't help answering in their head.
   - [The Enemy of Conventional Wisdom](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/disrupt/enemy-of-conventional-wisdom): name something your reader believes and argue with it.
2. **Say what Flowly actually does.** Use concrete nouns like tasks, docs, standups or handoffs, whatever is true.
3. **Put one piece of proof right next to the claim:** a customer count, a known logo or time saved.
4. **Make the button say what happens next.** "Start my free 14-day trial" tells people exactly what they're agreeing to. "Get Started" doesn't.
