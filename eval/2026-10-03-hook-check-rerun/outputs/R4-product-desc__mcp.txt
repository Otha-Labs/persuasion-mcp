**Shorter version for a product card:**
> The tee you'll still be reaching for next summer. Organic cotton that's soft from day one and built to outlast the $10 tees it replaces. Machine washable, true to size, six colors. $34.

**What changed and why**
- **The $34 price is now an argument.** People shopping for a basic tee compare it against cheap ones. The copy points out a cost they don't usually add up, which is buying the same cheap shirt again and again ([Hidden Cost Reveal](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/agitate/hidden-cost-reveal)). That makes the higher price read as the better deal.
- **The opening describes what owning it is like.** Instead of starting with a list of features, it starts with the shirt you always grab first ([Aspirational Identity](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/elevate/aspirational-identity)).
- **"Honestly, it's a plain T-shirt"** admits something unflattering on purpose ([Self-Deprecating Disclosure](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/bond/self-deprecating-disclosure)). The diagnostic flagged that the first draft never gave readers a reason to trust the brand, and an honest admission like this makes the durability claim easier to believe.
- **Fit and care answer the usual doubts.** "Will it fit?" and "Is it a pain to wash?" are handled before the button ([Preemptive Objection](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/resolve/preemptive-objection)).

**I didn't make up any facts, so here's what's left to fill in:**
1. **A specific sustainability fact.** "Sustainably made" on its own gets skimmed, and the FTC's Green Guides warn against vague green claims. A certification like GOTS or the name of your factory would make it believable.
2. **A return or fit guarantee, if you have one.** It's the strongest thing you could add, because it takes away most of the risk of buying.
3. **The headline promises the shirt lasts about a year.** Make sure your durability backs that up.
