## Built for people whose workday runs long

You're out the door by 7:30. If MoPac cooperates, you're home by 6:30. That's 11 hours, and your dog spends most of them on the couch, listening for your keys.

So we plan our schedule around yours. Your dog gets walked in the middle of your workday at the same time every day, which is when they need it most.

## You've probably tried to fix this already

Going home at lunch works until a meeting runs over. The neighbor's kid was great for about two weeks. And the app walker? Usually showed up.

But even when it worked, you still wondered: did my dog actually get a walk, or just five minutes in the yard?

## A walk you can't see is a walk you take on faith

Most dog walking runs on trust and a text that says "done!" That's fine until you start to wonder. We'd rather you never have to, so every walk leaves a record you can check.

**The route.** GPS tracks the walk from your door and back. You see the map and the distance, plus exactly when we left and got back.

**The photos.** When the walk is over, photos of your dog from that walk show up on your phone, usually somewhere between your 1:00 and your 2:00.

Now compare that with doing it yourself. A lunch-hour trip home can take 40 minutes in traffic, all for a rushed 10-minute loop around the block before you hurry back to your desk. For $25, your dog gets three times the walk, and you get your lunch break back.

**Notes**

- **The main idea:** People who work long days already know dog walkers exist. What they worry about is whether the walk actually happened. Your GPS tracking and photos answer that, so the page is built around being able to check.
- **The 30-minute promise is optional.** It turns your GPS into a guarantee, which is a strong trust signal. Only keep it if you'll honor it. ([Extended Warranty Window as Proof](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/prove/extended-warranty-window-as-proof))
- **Keep the heat question.** Midday walks in an Austin August are a real worry for owners, and a clear policy answers it.
- **Only use claims that are true.** I didn't invent a deadline or say your spots are limited. If your midday slots really do fill up, saying so near the last button would give people a stronger reason to book now.
- **A second headline to test:** "Your dog gets a 30-minute walk at noon. You get the photos." The current headline leads with being able to check the walk. This one leads with how the day goes for the owner.
- **Other techniques used:** [Enumerated Failed Alternatives](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/agitate/enumerated-failed-alternatives) (the lunch trip, the neighbor's kid, the app walker), [Value Framing](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/reframe/value-framing) ($25 compared with driving home), [Future Pacing](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/elevate/future-pacing) (6:30 p.m.), and [Micro-Commitment Chain](https://taxonomy.coppica.com/compel/micro-commitment-chain) (asking for a meet-and-greet instead of a booking).
