I don't have your numbers, so this plan assumes the most common reason an Instagram shop goes flat. Monday's first task checks whether that's right.

## Why sales are probably flat

- **Your followers have already decided.** Most people who follow you and wanted a pot have bought one. Reposting to the same people won't change that.
- **Planters don't wear out.** Past buyers need a new reason to buy, like a new plant, a gift or a new batch. Right now they probably don't hear from you between purchases.
- **The fix has two parts.** Give your list a reason to buy again (the email), and reach people who've never seen you (the ad).

**Don't discount.** Holiday shopping starts in about six weeks. A sale in October teaches buyers to wait for Black Friday and makes handmade work look like it was overpriced.

On ad spend: work out what you keep from an average order after materials and shipping. That's the most you can pay per sale and still break even. For example, if you keep $35 on a $70 order, you can pay up to $35. One week at $175 won't prove much. It only tells you whether the hook is worth spending more on.

## The ad (Instagram Reel, plus a 4:5 still for the feed)

2. **2–5s:** Your hands drop it, plastic and all, into a glazed planter. *"No repotting. Drop it in."*

## Before anything goes out

- **Fill in every [bracket] with real facts:** batch size, customer quote and breakage policy. If you don't have a quote or a breakage policy, delete that line rather than making one up. If there's no new batch ready, change "new batch" to "what's on the shelf right now." Each pot being one of a kind still works as the reason to buy now.
- **Check that the drop-in idea works with your pots.** The inside opening has to fit the rim of a standard nursery pot. If a size doesn't fit, don't list it as fitting.
- **I checked both drafts with a copy-review tool and revised them once.** It found the email didn't make readers feel it was written for them. The fix was the "this weekend since about June" line and the opening line for past customers. It found the ad listed features but didn't show the reader's life after buying, and the maker wasn't in it. The fixes were the "a potter in [city] made it" line and writing the caption as you. No copy can be shown to win before it runs, which is why the Sunday resend uses a different subject line.
