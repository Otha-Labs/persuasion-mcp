**A headline test probably isn't worth running here.** A new headline on a checkout page usually changes conversion by single digits, if at all. In 5 weeks you can only reliably spot a 30%+ lift. A real 5–10% improvement would probably come back "no significant difference," and you'd drop a change that actually helped.

**Your two numbers don't quite fit together.** People who reach a checkout page usually buy at well over 2%. If 600/day and 2.1% are site-wide figures, the test should use only checkout-page visitors and their purchase rate. **Ways to make the test practical:**
- **Test a bigger difference.** Instead of rewording the headline, try two versions that answer a different buyer worry. For example, one could deal with trust ("Is this safe or legit?") and the other with hesitation ("What if I regret it?"). Bigger differences produce bigger effects, and the result tells you which worry matters more to your buyers.
- **Measure an earlier step with more traffic**, such as clicks through to the payment step, alongside purchases.
- **Accept that you'll only catch a large effect.** Then run it for the full 5 or 10 weeks.

**Rules either way:**
- Decide the run length before you start.
- Run whole weeks so weekdays and weekends are both included.
- Don't stop early because one day looks good. Checking results repeatedly and stopping on a good day is the most common way A/B tests mislead people.
