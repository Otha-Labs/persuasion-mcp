# Sourdough Without Fear
**For total beginners who are a little bit scared of their own starter.**

People baked sourdough for thousands of years without a kitchen scale, a thermometer, or a single YouTube video.

So why does it feel this hard?

Maybe you've watched a few tutorials. Maybe you bookmarked a recipe that told you to "build a levain at 100% hydration," stared at it, and quietly closed the tab. If so, this book was written for you.

Here's the problem. Most sourdough advice comes from experienced bakers writing for other experienced bakers, so it skips the questions beginners actually worry about. How do I know my starter is alive? What if I forget to feed it? Why is my loaf flat? Nobody answers those. You end up with a jar of goo you're nervous to touch and a bag of flour you're afraid to waste.

The good news is that your starter is far tougher than the internet makes it sound. Forget it in the fridge for a week and it will usually bounce back after a feeding or two. Even a flat first loaf is useful, because it shows you exactly what to change next time. This book teaches you how to read it.

### What the next two weeks look like

**Day 1.** Stir flour and water together in a jar. Five minutes.

**Days 2 to 6.** Feed it once a day. You'll probably see a burst of bubbles, then a quiet spell that convinces most beginners they've killed it. They haven't. The book explains why and tells you what to do.

**Around day 7 to 10.** Your starter doubles within a few hours of a feeding. That's your green light.

**The weekend after.** Mix, fold, shape, bake. You follow one recipe, with every step spelled out.
